## Practical contribution for a club newsletter or trade editor

### Start your club’s digital history with one trophy

A trophy cabinet can hold decades of competition history without offering an easy way to find it. A past captain’s name might appear on several cups. A competition’s earliest winners may be engraved around a base which is difficult to read without moving the trophy. The information exists, but it is hard to consult.

A manageable archive project starts with one trophy and a clear result: a checked list of winners which someone else at the club can understand and maintain.

Begin by recording the trophy’s name, competition and visible date range. Photograph the complete object, then each engraved area. Use overlapping views for lettering around a curve. Inspect the photographs while the trophy is still available; a bright reflection across a date is a reason to try another angle.

The next step is review. Whether the initial text is typed manually or proposed by AI, someone must compare it with the source. An inscription reading “J. Smith” does not by itself identify a particular member. A missing year does not prove that a competition was cancelled. Supporting honours books, result sheets and minutes can help, but unresolved questions should remain questions.

Agree who can approve publication. A working archive and a public honours board have different jobs: reviewers need evidence and editable records, while members need a clear presentation of the history the club is ready to share.

Finally, plan the handover. Export a copy of the reviewed records, retain the original photographs and write down who maintains the archive. The project should survive a change of secretary or committee.

Trophy Guru is one way to put that workflow into practice. Clubs upload trophy photographs, review the proposed names and years, and can publish an online board with views by year, trophy and person. The first trophy is free, with up to 12 saved photographs and no payment card required. AI assists with the reading; the club checks the history.

A public demonstration and practical project checklist are available at trophy.guru/demo and trophy.guru/trophy-archive-project-plan. The demonstration uses fictional records to show the interface, not a customer endorsement.
