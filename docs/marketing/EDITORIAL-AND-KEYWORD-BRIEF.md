Prepared 14 September 2026. This is an implementation and publishing brief, not a keyword-volume report. No paid keyword database was available, so search volumes, difficulty scores and estimated rankings have not been invented.

## What the evidence tells us

Simon’s Google Search Console screenshot confirms that the UK cataloguing guide appeared for “ocr trophies”. It does not establish that every public page is indexed. The query “genealogy service trophy club” appears to have different intent; do not build genealogy pages around four impressions. Moz’s US database showing zero keywords is not proof of exclusion from Google.

The phrase “digital honours boards” describes an established category in primary supplier and club material. [Creative Honour Boards](https://www.digital-honours-boards.co.uk/) and [Dinosign](https://www.dinosign.co.uk/digital-honours-board) show the display and online-board category. [Sherfield Oaks](https://members.sherfieldoaksgolf.co.uk/honours-2/digital-honours-boards/) publishes honours material on its own club site. These sources show relevance, not a measured number of searches. They are not Trophy Guru customers or endorsements.

Trophy Guru’s distinguishing workflow is **recover the historical data from photographs, review it, then share it**. Broad “trophy engraving” searches can concern buying engraving services; the site must make its transcription purpose clear. Concentrate on clubs with existing historical collections, initially UK golf clubs.

## Copy this brief into the upstream article publisher

Write for a club secretary, golf club manager, volunteer historian or committee member who has existing trophies with historical winner engravings. Trophy Guru helps them photograph source records, review AI-assisted proposed names and years, correct or add supported information, export CSV records, and publish an online honours board with year, trophy and person views.

The core promise is a practical route from existing engravings to reviewed, searchable history. Do not describe physical trophy sales, engraving new trophies, CRM billing, competition scoring, genealogy services or screen installation as Trophy Guru features.

The first trophy is free with up to 12 saved photographs and no payment card required. Additional credits currently cost £7.50 for one, £60 for ten, £250 for fifty, and from £525 for 150 or more, including VAT. Core credits are a one-time purchase and never expire. Optional Intelligent Golf integration is a separate £299 per club per year; check the live integration page for availability. Always verify prices against the live pricing page before publishing.

Never invent customer names, usage counts, endorsements, testimonials, independent awards, benchmark percentages, fixed cabinet-completion times, specialist model-training claims, hardware compatibility, security certifications, legal compliance guarantees or competitor comparisons. Never promise 100% accuracy. Human checking is necessary and can still miss an error. Do not claim software recovers information which is not visible in the available evidence.

Every article must answer a distinct question or provide a practical resource. Prefer an original checked example, a useful checklist, a genuine authorised case study or a documented comparison. Clearly identify fictional examples. Do not pad an article to a fixed word count or add a year merely to make it look current.

Link to the most relevant product or guide page in context and give a clear next step: explore `/demo`, use the project checklist, or try one trophy free. Keep existing article URLs when improving content. A buying guide from Trophy Guru must disclose that it is provider-written, not an independent “best software” ranking.

FAQ answers must agree with visible content and appear once. Remove unsupported claims from both body text and structured data. Cite external factual claims at their source; do not fabricate research, statistics, quotations or links. The article should remain useful if the reader does not buy the service.

## The next valuable evidence to create

1. **One authorised real club pilot.** Photograph and review a real trophy with permission. Record the actual number of entries, corrections, unresolved readings and time spent. Obtain permission before publishing a club’s name, photographs or quoted feedback. This is a future evidence request, not a claim that a pilot or permission already exists.
2. **A short product walkthrough.** Show capture, review and published board using explicitly fictional or authorised material. The current public demo demonstrates the board; it does not prove transcription accuracy.
3. **A committee handover example.** Show how a club keeps its source photographs, exported records and review notes organised. Do not imply CSV contains all images or provides a complete system backup.

Publish those when there is evidence to show. Another generic 4,000-word AI article is not the priority.

## What to measure

Use Google Search Console to inspect the primary pages and relevant queries. Compare equivalent date ranges and country filters. Record whether impressions spread beyond the UK guide to the commercial pages. Use the launch campaign’s consent-based demo, pricing and signup-click events to understand interest. Record completed first-trophy trials and paying clubs separately; click counts are not sales.
