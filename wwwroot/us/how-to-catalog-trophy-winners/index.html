A practical guide for US organizations

How to catalog the winners engraved on your trophies.

Many country clubs, schools, colleges and athletic programs have decades of champions recorded only on trophy cups, plaques and presentation awards. Those names can become a searchable digital catalog without treating an uncertain AI reading as fact.

The challenge

Why engraved trophies defeat ordinary text scanners

Trophy inscriptions are not flat, high-contrast documents. Chrome and silver reflect the room, curved nameplates distort letters, and years of polishing can soften the engraving. One photo can make an “S” resemble a “5” or hide the middle of a last name entirely.

A useful digital record therefore needs more than generic OCR. It should compare multiple views, show uncertainty, preserve the source photos and require a human decision before a champion is marked as confirmed.

A repeatable process

Six steps from trophy case to searchable catalog

  1. 01

    Inventory the awards

    Start with the award name, sport or competition, approximate date range and any record already held by the organization. Keep the full-trophy reference photos separate from close-up inscription evidence.

  2. 02

    Capture overlapping inscription photos

    Use a smartphone, soft side lighting and several angles. Move the camera or rotate the trophy when glare covers the text. Photograph every nameplate, base band and engraved face, with overlap between neighboring images.

  3. 03

    Let AI compare the whole batch

    Add several photos in one visit. Trophy Archive AI processes the combined evidence in the background, so volunteers can keep photographing instead of waiting after every upload.

  4. 04

    Check each champion and year

    Proposed readings appear as an editable chronological list with confidence information. Correct a name, change a season, reject a suggestion or return to the source image before confirming it.

  5. 05

    Resolve missing seasons and possible people

    Define the expected year range to surface gaps. Import a roster, alumni file or membership list to find likely people, then use birth year and the award year to exclude impossible matches. The user chooses when several candidates remain.

  6. 06

    Export and reuse the finished history

    Download a CSV containing awards, years, winners, review state and optional membership matches. Use it in a hall-of-fame page, printed history, anniversary project or another records system.

A better photo session

What your volunteers need

A phone with free storageA recent smartphone is enough. Several clear angles matter more than specialist camera equipment.

Indirect, adjustable lightAvoid pointing the flash directly at reflective metal. Move the light and camera until different parts of the engraving become legible.

An award inventoryCompetition names and approximate year ranges help identify duplicate awards and missing seasons.

Supporting historical sourcesYearbooks, newsletters, meeting minutes and newspaper clippings can settle a name the metal no longer reveals.

It can document cups, plaques, bowls, shields, medals, perpetual awards and engraved championship boards.

Common questions

What happens when the evidence is incomplete?

Can we add a missing winner from a yearbook or newsletter?

Yes. Set the expected year range, identify the gap and add a winner manually from a source your organization trusts.

What if two membership records match the same initials?

Both candidates can remain visible with membership number and age at the time of the award. A user selects the correct person or removes the match entirely.

Does the software overwrite a confirmed name?

No. Human-confirmed winners are not silently replaced by a later AI reading.
