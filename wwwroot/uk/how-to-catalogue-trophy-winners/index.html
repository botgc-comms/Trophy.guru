A practical guide for UK clubs

How to catalogue the winners engraved on your trophies.

If the only complete record of your competition winners is engraved on cups, shields and presentation trophies, the history is recoverable. The safest method is to preserve the photographic evidence, use AI to propose names and years, and keep a person in control of the final archive.

The problem

Why ordinary OCR is not enough for trophy engraving

Polished silver, curved bands, shallow lettering, tarnish and reflections make engraved trophies difficult for an ordinary document scanner. A single photograph may hide part of a surname or turn one digit into another. The reliable approach is to compare several overlapping views and keep each proposed reading connected to the photographs that produced it.

That matters because a plausible name is not necessarily the correct name. Your club should be able to inspect uncertain letters, edit the result and confirm the record before it becomes part of the permanent winners list.

Step by step

A six-step trophy cataloguing method

  1. 01

    List the trophies before you start

    Record the trophy name, competition, approximate first year and any notes already held by the club. Photograph the complete trophy separately from the close-up evidence used to read its engraving.

  2. 02

    Photograph every engraved band

    Use a phone and indirect light. Move around the trophy, overlap adjacent photographs and take extra views wherever glare covers a letter. A gentle rubbing can be uploaded as complementary evidence when the metal is especially difficult.

  3. 03

    Analyse the complete image set

    Upload several images at once. Trophy Archive AI waits for the set, then compares the angles in the background instead of forcing you to stop after every photograph.

  4. 04

    Review names, dates and confidence

    The proposed winners are placed in chronological order. You can correct spelling, change a year or reject a reading while the original photographs remain available for checking.

  5. 05

    Find gaps and reconcile member records

    Set the expected date range to reveal missing years. Where a membership export is available, compare the engraved name with likely members and use birth year to rule out chronologically impossible matches.

  6. 06

    Confirm and export the archive

    Only a person at the club confirms the final list. Export the trophy details, years, winners, review state and optional member matches as CSV for the website, honours book or back-office records.

Before photographing

What to have beside the trophy cabinet

A charged phoneModern phone cameras are sufficient; a professional camera is not required.

Soft, movable lightAvoid a direct flash reflected in polished metal. Change the angle rather than relying on one perfect shot.

A simple trophy listNames and approximate date ranges make gaps easier to identify later.

Any supporting recordsMinutes, honours boards and old yearbooks can resolve inscriptions that remain ambiguous.

Who it is for

Golf, rugby, cricket, bowls and more

The same process works for golf clubs, rugby and cricket clubs, tennis and bowls clubs, schools, universities, associations, museums and local societies. It also works for cups, shields, salvers, medals, plaques and honours boards wherever names and dates form a repeated historical record.

Common questions

Practical answers before you begin

Can we add a winner when the trophy has a missing year?

Yes. Missing years can be added manually from minutes, yearbooks, newspaper archives or another trusted club record.

Does an AI reading become an official record automatically?

No. Every proposed name and date remains editable until a person at the club confirms it.

Can member information help resolve initials?

Yes. A member export can produce likely candidates, show membership numbers and calculate age at the time of the award. Ambiguous candidates remain a choice for the user.
